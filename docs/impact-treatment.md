# Seed to Shelf: the AI impact case film

Creative treatment for the 2:40 case film (1920×1080, 30 fps, stereo 48 kHz) and its 1:22 teaser.
Deliverables: `out/eagerworks-ai-impact.mp4` and `out/eagerworks-ai-impact-teaser.mp4` (burned-in captions).

---

## 1. Central idea

**Impact isn't a promise. It's a formula.** Every AI project starts by defining how its impact will be measured.

The film follows **one fresh-produce grower's season, from seed to shelf**, through six AI cases, each closing
on its **impact formula**. The grower is never named; no client data appears.

| Station | Case | Formula (symbolic, no numbers) |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Nursery | Seedling survival (proposal) | Seedlings saved × Cost per seedling |
| 02 Field | Harvest forecast (lettuce) | Boxes not wasted × Cost / box + Shortfalls avoided × Penalty |
| 03 Harvest | Onion sizing on the windrower (proposal) | Sampling hours saved × Cost / hour + Tonnes in the right band × Price difference |
| 04 Packhouse | Pumpkin count & quality | Hours saved × Cost / hour + Kg graded right × Price difference |
| 05 Dispatch | Crate labeling | Rejections avoided × (Fine + Product + Freight) |
| 06 Office | Dashboard, reports, alerts & AI assistant | Reporting hours saved × Cost / hour |

The order follows the value chain (nursery → field → harvest → packhouse → dispatch → office), so the season reads as
one story.

**Viewer takeaway:** *"Eagerworks builds AI whose impact you can measure, across the whole operation."*

### Recurring devices

1. **The violet line**: the signature of the AI Studio film. It becomes the value chain with six stations. Between
   chapters, every scene collapses into one standard line (`CHAIN`, y = 540), and the next scene turns it into its
   own horizon, rail, belt or slab. Because every scene enters and exits the same way, any order works, so the teaser
   reuses the scenes as they are.
2. **The impact equation**: the hero moment of each chapter.
   - The scene dims and a `MEASURED IMPACT` formula builds token by token, each token appearing when the voiceover
     names it.
   - Some terms fly out of the scene itself: the coral surplus becomes "Boxes not wasted", and the "One rejection
     costs" chips become the bracket.
   - `= IMPACT` lands on a chime.
   - The formula then docks into its station on the chain map.
3. **The chain map** (top right): six dots. Coral means a leak not yet fixed, violet means fixed. Each dot turns
   violet as its formula docks.
4. **Proposal cards** (nursery, onions): *Need | Solution* in the look of Eagerworks' proposal slides (black, blue
   corner glow, wide display titles, Manrope body with highlighted phrases, logo). The text is anonymized. The
   *Solution* column dissolves into the working system.

> A seedling that never comes up. A harvest that misses the order. A crate at the wrong store.
> Impact isn't a promise. It's a formula.
> At Eagerworks, every AI project starts by defining how its impact will be measured.
> Here's one grower's season, from seed to shelf.
>
> It starts in the nursery. Cameras over the moving trays count and size every seedling, and flag weak trays early.
> More seedlings survive: less replanting, fewer days with an empty field.
> The impact: seedlings saved, times the cost of each one.
>
> In the field, a camera on the tractor measures every lettuce. The model forecasts the boxes ready each day, and matches them to the orders.
> Less product thrown away. Fewer short deliveries.
> The impact: boxes not wasted, times their cost, plus shortfalls avoided, times the penalty.
>
> At harvest, every field needs its size split.
> Off-the-shelf models missed onions, so a custom model over the windrower counts each one once, and sorts it by size.
> The impact: sampling hours saved, times their cost, plus tonnes in the right size band, times the price difference.
>
> In the packhouse, every pumpkin is counted and graded for quality, with no hand counts. So each one sells in the right class, at the right price.
> The impact: hours saved, times cost per hour, plus kilos graded right, times the price difference.
>
> At dispatch, one crate at the wrong supermarket means a rejection, and a fine.
> AI recognizes each crate, checks the order, labels it, and holds back any mismatch. Fewer rejections, returns and lost product.
> The impact: rejections avoided, times the fine, plus the product, plus the freight.
>
> Finally, it all lands in one place. One data foundation powers a live dashboard, self-building reports, and alerts while there's still time to act.
> Ask in plain English, and the answer comes from live data.
> The impact: reporting hours saved, times the cost per hour.
>
> Six problems. Six formulas. One season.
> Because AI is only worth building when you can measure what it gives back.
> Eagerworks AI Studio. Let's find your formula.

## 4. Visual direction

- **Palette:** the AI Studio film's palette, from Eagerworks' white-on-black logo.
  - Graphite base, warm white text.
  - Violet `#8234FE` → blue `#6686F6` marks what the AI sees, infers or produces.
  - Coral marks leaks and problems, amber partial issues.
  - Proposal cards use the proposal slides' black with a blue corner glow.
  - The dashboard is a cream "paper" screen floating on graphite. Size bands run blue → green → amber → orange.
- **Typography:** the Eagerworks pairing.
  - *Archivo* at 118–125 % width for display type. It matches the proposals' titles; a test render was compared
    against them.
  - *Manrope* for interface, body and tracked uppercase labels. Its use in the proposal decks was checked in the PDF
    and the Figma styles.
- **Look:** a premium dark SaaS film, matching the AI Studio film: matte panels, hairline borders, an engineering
  grid, vignette and fine grain. Glow is reserved for AI signals and the formula.
- **Reused from the AI Studio film:** the line-art tractor (moved to `video/src/lib/tractor.js`, pixel-identical in
  the original film), the field sprites, the pumpkin line (sprites, belt, rails, IDs, count line), the HUD and
  stepper, detection overlays, and the logo build.

## 5. Sound

Same engine (`audio/synth.py`), 100 BPM, A minor / C major.
- The open is sparse, with impacts on each leak, a riser into the collapse hit, a hit on "formula" and a swell on the
  lockup.
- Chapters: the nursery is soft (bells, shaker), the field mechanical, the onion harvest the peak (16th bass, stabs),
  the packhouse a mechanical groove, dispatch tense in Dm9 while the leak plays and resolving to C on the fix, and
  the office lighter.
- The close drops, rises and resolves on the logo.
- Each formula token rings a bell (ascending pentatonic), `= IMPACT` lands on a chime, and docking plays a suck and a
  lock. These live in `sfx.py`, so the ducked music doesn't swallow them.
- New sounds: the REJECTED stamp, a label printer, a pneumatic applicator, and the rattle of the windrower's rods.

## 6. Anonymization and accuracy rules

- **The client is never named or hinted.** There are no company names, locations, real field or grower names,
  retailer brands (stores are `STORE A/B/C`), or figures from the client's dashboards or proposals.
- **The formulas are symbolic.** There are no currency amounts, percentages, accuracy figures or savings claims.
  Counters, kg, field names (North 4, Weir 2, Hill 7…) and dates inside the interfaces are invented demo content.
- **Proposals are shown as proposals.** Nursery and onions carry *Proposal · Need & Solution* cards, and the
  voiceover describes what the systems do without claiming a deployment.
- **The only client-origin image is the onion proof-of-concept photo.** It comes from the proposal (707×391, no
  identifying content, metadata stripped). The nursery proposal's tray photos were not used, because they come from
  other vendors' trials.
- **The three HTML dashboards were references for content types only.** Field maps cell by cell, size distributions,
  size splits and planners inspired the dashboard; its layout, palette and data are new.
